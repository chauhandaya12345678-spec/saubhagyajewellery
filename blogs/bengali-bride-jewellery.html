Bengali bride jewellery: champakali, tikli and the right jhumka

A Bengali bride wears tradition in two layers. The first is the shakha and pola - the white and red bangles given by her parents - which stay on through the ceremony. The second is the bridal set: a champakali or chik necklace that hugs the throat, a tikli at the hair parting, and long jhumkas that echo the chik design.

For brides outside Bengal who want the look, the shape matters more than the origin. A champakali-style necklace is built from small dome-shaped petals in gold tone, and it sits beautifully over a red or white Banarasi border. The tikli replaces the traditional maang tikka, resting flat on the forehead instead of hanging.

Choosing the earrings

Bengali jhumkas are traditionally long, brushing the collarbone, so they show when the pallu is pinned. If the necklace is busy, pick a simpler jhumka; if the neck is bare, let the earrings carry the look. Our gold-plated jhumka earrings come in sizes that work for both sangeet and the main ceremony.

Keeping it light for saat paake

The saat paake ends the night, so the jewellery should already be comfortable. Two-piece sets - necklace plus jhumkas - cover the whole ceremony without the weight of a full haaram. Every Saubhagya piece is gold-plated over a light base, designed to stay on from morning till the final pheras.
