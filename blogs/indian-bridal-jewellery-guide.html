Complete Indian Bridal Jewellery Guide 2026

An Indian bride's jewellery is never just accessories — every piece carries tradition, symbolism and generations of craftsmanship. Whether you are shopping for yourself or helping a friend, here is a piece-by-piece guide to building a complete bridal set.

Maang Tikka

Sits at the centre parting of the hair, falling onto the forehead. The maang tikka represents the third eye and is traditionally the first piece a bride puts on. Choose a design that complements your matha patti or wear it solo for a minimalist bridal look. Hook-back styles are more secure than paste-on ones for long ceremonies.

Rani Haar & Long Necklace

The statement piece. A rani haar hangs below the bust line and pairs beautifully with a deep-neck blouse. For imitation bridal jewellery, opt for high gold-plated Zamak alloy — it mimics the weight and drape of real gold without the cost. Layer it with a shorter choker for the classic South Indian bridal stack.

Choker

Sits snug at the base of the neck. A kundan or polki-style choker frames the face beautifully in photographs. Budget-friendly tip: a well-made gold-plated choker costs ₹500-800 while a real gold equivalent would be 40-50 times that.

Jhumkas & Earrings

The heavier the jhumka, the more regal the look — but make sure the weight is supported by an ear chain, not just the lobe. Temple-style jhumkas with peacock or Lakshmi motifs are perennially popular for South Indian brides. Expect to spend ₹200-400 for a good gold-plated pair.

Nath (Nose Ring)

A traditional Maharashtrian and North Indian bridal element. Large naths connect to the hair with a chain for support. If a full nath feels too heavy, a simple stud with a pearl drop is a modern alternative that still photographs well.

Bajuband (Armlet)

Worn on the upper arm, a bajuband adds a royal touch — especially with sleeveless or cap-sleeve blouses. Most bridal sets include a matching pair. Opt for adjustable chains so it stays put through the ceremony.

Kamarbandh (Waist Chain)

A kamarbandh cinches the saree at the waist and is especially striking with Kanjeevaram silks. Temple-design kamarbandhs with coin drops are trending in 2026. Gold-plated versions start around ₹600.

Anklets & Toe Rings

Silver-toned anklets (payal) are traditional for brides — the gentle sound is considered auspicious. Toe rings (bichua) are worn on both feet as a symbol of marital status in Hindu tradition.

Budget Guide (Imitation Bridal Set)

A complete high-quality gold-plated bridal set — maang tikka, rani haar, choker, jhumkas, nath, bajuband, kamarbandh and anklets — can be assembled for ₹3,000-5,000. That is less than one gram of real gold today. And the best part: no security guard needed at the wedding.
