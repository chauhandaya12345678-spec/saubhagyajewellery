Kundan vs Polki: what brides should actually compare

Kundan settings hold flat, foiled glass stones in gold-toned frames for a formal, structured sparkle; Polki-style pieces use uncut-look stones for a raw, older-world glow. For daytime weddings, Polki photographs softer; for receptions under artificial light, Kundan throws more fire. Match the base tone to your outfit borders, not your skin tone — that is what shows in photos.

Here is a simple way to decide: if your lehenga has zari-heavy embroidery, choose Kundan so the sparkle competes evenly with the fabric. If your outfit is matte or pastel, Polki's soft glow complements rather than overwhelms it. Either way, keep the metal tone consistent across the set — necklace, earrings, maang tikka — so the bridal look reads as one piece.
