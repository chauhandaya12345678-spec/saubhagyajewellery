Maharashtrian jewellery: nath, kolhapuri saaj and tanmani

Maharashtrian jewellery is recognisable from across the room: the nath draped from the left nostril, the kolhapuri saaj hugging the throat in rows, and the tanmani pendant at the centre of the neckline. Paired with a paithani or nauvari saree, the set reads instantly as festive.

The nath is the signature piece. Traditional designs range from a simple gold ring to a pear-shaped piece with pearls and small stones. If a full traditional nath feels heavy, lighter gold-plated versions keep the look without the strain of a long evening.

The kolhapuri saaj and tanmani

The saaj is a choker made of multiple parallel rows that widen slightly toward the centre. The tanmani is the small pendant piece at the front. Together they fill the neckline of a saree blouse perfectly - which is why a saaj-style choker works even when the blouse is heavily embroidered.

Festive styling beyond the wedding

You do not need to be a bride to wear Maharashtrian jewellery. A tanmani pendant over a plain cotton or silk saree lifts a Diwali lunch or a family function instantly. Gold-plated options keep the whole set light and easy to store, and they pair well with our short necklace and pendant styles.
