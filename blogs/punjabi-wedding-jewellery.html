Punjabi wedding jewellery: what a bride actually wears

A Punjabi bridal look is built in layers: the red and ivory chooda with kalire, a maang tikka sitting at the parting, a choker or raani haar at the neck, and long jhumkas that swing with the lehenga. The rule is fullness, not heaviness - the baraat and pheras run for hours, so weight matters more than sparkle.

Start with the earrings. Jhumka and chandbali styles sit well with the high Punjabi bun and the dupatta pinned at the shoulder. Kundan and meenakari finishes photograph clearly against red, maroon and gold lehengas, while antique-gold tones suit pastel and ivory outfits better.

Layering the neckline

A single heavy necklace can feel like too much by the second hour. Most Punjabi brides wear one structured choker close to the neck, then let the dupatta and blouse do the rest. If the blouse is embroidered, keep the necklace simple; if it is plain, a wider raani haar balances the frame.

Matching the set

Keep the metal tone consistent - all gold or all antique - so the set reads as one piece in photos. Our bridal and festive necklaces pair naturally with jhumka earrings from the same family, and every piece is gold-plated so the full look stays light enough for a full day of functions.
