South Indian temple jewellery: jhumkas, kasulaperu and kemp

Temple jewellery takes its name from the motifs carved on temple walls - gods, peacocks, lotus and gopuram shapes - pressed into gold-tone metal. South Indian brides layer it over kanjeevaram silk, but the pieces work for any festive occasion once you know which shapes to pick.

The temple jhumka is the easiest entry point. Domes of embossed gold with a peacock or lotus motif and a small bell-shaped drop catch the light against a deep red or green silk saree. They suit both a full bridal look and a simple puja outfit.

Kasulaperu and the coin necklace

Kasulaperu is the classic South Indian coin necklace - linked gold coins that sit flat across the collarbone. A kasulaperu-style chain fills the neckline without the weight of a solid haaram, which makes it the practical choice for long ceremonies. Gold-plated versions stay light and tarnish-resistant with basic care.

Kemp stones and the red accent

Kemp, the deep red stone set in gold, is the traditional South Indian accent. A kemp-studded piece adds the bridal red without relying on heavy rubies. If the saree border is red or gold, a kemp or stone-set piece ties the whole outfit together; if the border is plain, keep the jewellery to solid gold tones.
